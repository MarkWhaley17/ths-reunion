Planning a Reunion

Your reunion can be whatever you want it to be!  It can be simple, elaborate or anywhere in between.  There is a wealth of information on the internet explaining how to plan a reunion (some recommended sites are listed at the end of this document.)  There are many options to choose from, and decisions to make, and it can be daunting.  But it doesn’t have to be!  Don’t let it overwhelm you!

The best thing to do is envision the type of reunion you want to hold and work toward that goal.  The most important thing you can do to ensure a successful reunion is to contact as many classmates as you possibly can, get them excited about the reunion, and get them to spread the word.  Where to hold the reunion, what type of food to have, what to charge, decorations; these decisions are secondary to getting the word out to your class.

The reason people come to a reunion is to have a good time and visit with their old classmates.  Instead of trying to wow them with fancy food, elaborate decorations, and costly entertainment, plan a nice location, with good food and drink, for a reasonable price, and your reunion will be a success.

General Planning

The Committee

It is okay if you are the only one on the committee, but it is a lot of work, even for a simple reunion.  We recommend getting a few people (but not too many) to help.  It is advantageous, but not necessary, to get people from different social groups (athletics, band, theatre, academics) as each will have a different group of friends, which will make networking the news out to your class easier.  It is also advantageous to get people who have different skills (computer, catering, decorating, money management) to help organize the reunion.

Social Media

If your class doesn’t already have one, set up a group Facebook (Instagram, etc.) page now! Start getting the word out for people to join.  We recommend you name the page ‘Towson High School Class of ####’.  That way you can use it throughout the years for many purposes.  Check out other Facebook pages for similar classes, although many are private.  ‘Towson High School Class of 1973’ is public and very active (https://www.facebook.com/groups/TowsonHS1973/.)

Warning: Many classes have made the mistake of thinking a social media page would do all the work for them.  It doesn’t, and it won’t.  It is only one tool that you will need to try to locate and communicate with your classmates.

Your Vision

Planning your reunion involves a lot of decisions.  What do you want the vibe to be?  Casual, formal, or somewhere in between.  Indoors or outdoors?   A happy hour, cocktail party, dinner, or picnic?  Catered food or a-la-carte from a menu?   Open bar, cash bar or beer &amp; wine?   Spring, summer, or fall?

Here are some brief examples of the range:

Easy: Select a bar, restaurant, or hotel lounge.  Give your class the date and time to meet.  Tell them that all food and drink are at their expense.  Contact the venue beforehand to let them know that you are planning this type of reunion.  You need to make sure they have the appropriate staff available.  They might even offer reduced food and drink prices for bringing in such a crowd.  Keep them up to date as far as the number of people expected.

Typical: Select a bar, restaurant, or country club.  Plan a date and time.  Work with the venue on the menu, having a ticket price in mind as a guideline.  Don’t be afraid to ask for less expensive options.  Open Bar will drive your price way up.  Consider beer and wine only, or a Cash Bar.

Elaborate:    A multi-day, multi-event reunion, including: a meet and greet happy hours on Friday night, a dinner/dance on Saturday night and a buffet breakfast Sunday morning.  Arrange for a block of hotel rooms.  This requires a lot of planning!  Unless you are super energetic with tons of help and have prior reunion experience, we do not recommend it for beginners!

Towson High Alumni Advertising

Let the Alumni Association know that you are planning a reunion so that they can advertise on their website and in the bi-annual Newsnote newsletter.  Even before you know the specifics, you can advertise that a reunion is being planned and list your contact information and the class social media sites.  Get the word out early!

Class List

Once the planning has begun for your reunion, we can provide you with a list of names and addresses for your class.  The list will also include the phone numbers and email addresses that we have.  We highly recommend that you mail (snail mail) an invitation to everyone in your class once the details are known.  This is costly, but well worth it.  It is typically the only way to reach everyone.

Banking

You will need to decide how you are going to handle the money.  Some classes open a bank account for the reunion.  Some classes use their personal bank accounts.   Will you except checks, Venmo payments, online payment?  These are questions that you should start thinking about early.

Specific Planning

When

The most popular time to hold a reunion is in the fall.  Probably because people don’t start thinking about planning until the year of the reunion and it takes until fall to pull it together.  Obviously Friday and Saturday nights work the best.  Beware of July and August as so many people are on vacation.  June or September are good picnic choices.    The Friday after Thanksgiving is popular for recent graduates as they tend to be home with their parents for the holiday.

Where

Bar, restaurant, country club, hotel, swim club, park or point of interest.  They all work.  See the list of venues that have been used by other classes at the end of this document.  Give the venue an estimate of the number of people you expect, but do not lock in or sign a contract for a pre-determined number of people.  Most reputable venues understand and will work with you as the reunion grows.

Food and Drink

Offer enough food options so that people have a choice, without going overboard.  Adding an open bar to the ticket price is very costly.  Consider offering beer and wine with a cash bar.

Music

Consider a band, or DJ.  However, a playlist of music that was popular in high school is just as good.  People are coming to socialize.  You don’t want a band or DJ to be so loud that people can’t talk to each other.  The venue may be able to play a channel of music from your generation.

Decorations

Decorations are totally up to you but can be costly.  Balloons are a nice, inexpensive option.   Potted flowers (petunias, mums, poinsettias) are also nice.  Often the venue will have seasonal decorations.  Check with them before you buy your own.

Postage

We highly recommend mailing everyone in your class.  It is often the only way to reach them.  This is a significant expense that typically needs to be paid before any registration money comes in.  If there were 300 graduates in your class and a stamp is 73 cents (56 cents for postcard), the postage will cost $219.  This doesn’t include paper, envelopes or copying expenses.

Other Expenses

In addition to decorations and postage, don’t forget office supplies and bank charges (if accepting online payments).  Do your best not to overcharge (because it will turn some people off) but do not undercharge either (or you will be stuck with shortage).   We recommend adding at least $5 to each ticket to cover expenses.  If you are going to give away mementos, you may need to add even more.

Price

The ticket price should be in line with the venue and what you are offering.  You are not going to get a country club to offer a great meal and open bar for $40.  On the other hand, if you charge $100 for hamburgers, hotdogs, and sodas at a picnic, nobody will come.

Depending on what you offer, the ticket price could range from $30-$100.   The average cost of a nice reunion with good food and beer and wine is probably between $50 and $70.  The cost will go up if the food is elaborate or if an open bar is included.   The cost will be lower if you limit the food offerings or specify a cash bar.  And remember to factor in your expenses.    If you are not sure if the price is on target, ask yourself, “Is this a fair price for what is being offered?’  and ‘Would I buy the ticket?’

Registration Options

Decide whether you will require people to pay in advance, pay-at-the door, or both.  When people pay in advance, it makes it easier on you.  You know who is coming and you know how much you will need to pay for the venue.  However, many people are last-minute decision makers and paying at the door is an attractive option for them.  Allowing people to pay at the door can add a significant number of attendees to your reunion.  If you are going to offer both, encourage people to pay early by offering an early registration price of $55 and an at-the-door price of $60 or $65.

Having your reunion be 100% pay at the door can be risky.  You will never know who is really going to show up.   If you have put out your own money for a buffet or decorations for example, you may never get your money back.

Finding and Notify Classmates

This is the most important thing to do.  Use Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, snail mail, email, local papers, phone calls, internet searches, word-of-mouth -- whatever it takes to locate and inform people.   We cannot emphasis this enough.   A successful reunion will not just ‘happen’.  You need to track people down and get them talking.  Don’t just focus on your friends.  Make sure you are reaching out to the entire class!   The most important bit of information to get is their email address.  If they join the Facebook page, ask them to email you as well so that you can capture their address.  Always ask for their email address.   Always provide them with your email and phone number as well.

Database/Spreadsheet Tracking

You must have a method for documenting and tracking your classmates.  Spreadsheets or databases work well.  In addition to permanent information like name, address, phone, and email, you will want to keep information regarding the current reunion like number of tickets, amount paid, date paid and method of payment (check/venmo/etc.) for example.

It is also a good idea to note whether you have been in contact with them, or whether they are missing.  You may want to email the list of missing classmates to those you have been in contact with so that they can help you find them.

As we mentioned above, the Alumni Association can provide you with a list of your class once preparations for the reunion have begun.
